\section{Conclusion}

MIDI-Instruct defines instruction-conditioned symbolic music editing as a benchmarkable task with dual edit and preserve metrics, a separate MIDI-Reason plan track, and an open synthetic dataset generator. The oracle's 1.0 joint score and the zero joint scores of copy-source and wrong-transform controls validate the intended metric boundaries. A Llama-3.2-1B plan-then-execute baseline reaches 0.44 joint success, with perfect muting and tempo edits but systematic failures on velocity scaling, program changes, negative transposition, and structured output validity. These operator-level differences would be obscured by validity, preservation, or plan accuracy alone. By grading emitted MIDI with programmatic predicates rather than LLM judges and retaining failed attempts in the denominator, the benchmark supports reproducible diagnosis of future text-to-MIDI systems, editing models, and tool-augmented agents. The next release should broaden composition diversity, add constraint-gold instructions, and compare constrained decoding and tool-augmented runners.
