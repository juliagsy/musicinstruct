\section{Results}

\subsection{Overall comparison (v0.2 real MIDI)}

Table~\ref{tab:overall-results} reports all systems on the v0.2 test split: 180 items from 45 MidiCaps/Lakh compositions, subsampled to four operator families per composition. The oracle---which emits reference gold MIDI and plans---reaches joint pass rate 0.933 with validity 1.0. The gap below 1.0 reflects predicate edge cases on diverse real files (e.g., tempo estimation on rubato sources and preservation checks after program change), not model error; the synthetic pilot oracle reaches joint 1.0 (Table~\ref{tab:control-results}). Copy source attains perfect \preserve{} but joint 0.022: four program-change items receive accidental partial edit credit when the unchanged source already satisfies a predicate. Wrong transform produces valid MIDI with mean \edit{} 0.163 and \preserve{} 0.925 but zero joint passes. No output scores zero across all axes.

The Llama plan-then-execute baseline obtains joint pass rate 0.589 (106 of 180 items). Mean \edit{} is 0.633 and mean \preserve{} is 0.658. Validity is 0.744: 46 items (25.6\%) produce no executable MIDI, scored as invalid rather than omitted. Exact plan match is 0.622. Composition-macro joint equals the item micro-average (0.589) because every test composition contributes exactly four equally weighted items. Llama therefore exceeds copy source and wrong transform on \edit{} and joint, but remains far below oracle and still loses roughly one quarter of items to invalid structured output.

\subsection{Performance by operation}

Performance varies sharply by operator family for Llama on v0.2 (Table~\ref{tab:operator-results}). Tempo scaling is solved perfectly (45/45 joint). Track muting reaches 0.889 joint with full validity. Program change remains difficult: joint 0.222 and validity 0.733, with many responses mapping General MIDI program numbers to transpose plans. Transposition is the weakest family: joint 0.244 and validity 0.244, driven by truncated JSON, hallucinated track lists, and out-of-range track indices on short real files.

\subsection{Failure analysis}

Forty-six Llama predictions produce no MIDI on v0.2. Thirty-eight responses fail JSON parsing under the 256-token cap (\texttt{no\_plan\_returned}): many begin valid transpose objects but truncate mid-\texttt{tracks} array, while program-change items sometimes emit unbounded enumerations of track indices. Eight additional failures reference out-of-range tracks (e.g., track~25 on two-track files), often from mapping program~25 to a transpose interval. Among the 134 executable outputs, semantic errors include wrong operator choice (transpose instead of \texttt{program\_change}), default program~0 instead of the requested patch, and correct semitone counts with excess tracks that still pass or fail depending on drum masking.

The comparison illustrates why both axes are needed. Copy source on v0.2 attains perfect \preserve{} but near-zero joint (Table~\ref{tab:overall-results}), while the model's invalid or misdirected edits lower preservation to 0.658 and raise OverEdit to 0.340. Wrong transform stays at joint 0 despite partial edit and preservation credit, separating ``some change occurred'' from ``the requested edit succeeded under both axes.'' File-level predicates therefore remain the primary task metric, with MIDI-Reason reported as a diagnostic track.
