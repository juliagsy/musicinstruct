\begin{abstract}
Instruction-conditioned music editing is the natural next step after text-to-audio generation, yet symbolic benchmarks still emphasize either theory question answering over score images or audio remixing without MIDI-native preservation checks. We introduce \textbf{MIDI-Instruct}, a benchmark of source MIDI, natural-language instructions, and machine-checkable edit specifications. Each item is scored on two independent axes: \edit{} (did the requested transformation occur?) and \preserve{} (did unmentioned musical attributes remain unchanged?). A companion \textbf{MIDI-Reason} track evaluates structured edit plans separately from emitted MIDI files, exposing the same knowledge--action gap observed in agent benchmarks: a model may state the correct operation while producing the wrong file. Version~0.1 comprises approximately 300 unique-gold items synthesized from multi-track seeds via invertible transforms (transpose, tempo and velocity scaling, track muting, program change), with programmatic predicates replacing LLM judges for headline metrics. We release the dataset generator, grader, and evaluation harness.
\end{abstract}
